## Findings in brief

1. [I] Make weather a versioned, deterministic **world field**, with a point sampler and a volume representation. Data feeds describe that field; JSBSim and rendering consume it. Rendering quality, camera position and network completion order must never change aircraft weather.
2. [O/P] METARs are accessible from the terminal but **AviationWeather.gov explicitly disallows CORS**. The static sites need imported files, a separately operated proxy, or public snapshots prepared before publication. Open-Meteo forecast and historical JSON, NOAA's public S3 model objects and NASA GIBS responded with permissive CORS in the tested requests.
3. [I] Start with manual/seeded profiles, temperature/pressure and visibility, followed by coherent cloud layers. This makes weather usable and testable on every backend before detailed clouds. Live data is optional and introduces privacy, terms, historical-data and availability decisions.
4. [O/P] Installed JSBSim already accepts point temperature/pressure overrides as well as three wind components. It can receive a spatial field each step without a new engine API. Its turbulence has material defects: type 1 does nothing, Dryden height uses ASL where its near-ground formulation needs AGL, pure east/west wind leaves a stale heading, and switching the type off leaves angular turbulence active. Microbursts are unfinished.
5. [I] Prefer Dryden Tustin for a bounded, validated aircraft turbulence option; establish its spectrum and transition behaviour before shipping it. It is an aircraft trajectory process, not a spatial cloud wind field. Do not count procedural gusts and JSBSim turbulence twice.
6. [O/P/I] The FJ33 schedule uses both density altitude and `delta-T`. Arbitrary point temperatures do not update the latter. The concurrent engine redesign must specify one consistent ambient contract; weather must not silently bypass the existing hot-day correction.
7. [I] Clouds, fog, rain, shadows and exposure must share occupancy, extinction and incident light. A cheap layer can provide correct entry/exit and attenuation even when it cannot draw detailed billows. WebGL 2 can ray march in a fragment shader; WebGPU is not a mathematical prerequisite.
8. [I] A proposal can be drafted with explicit user decisions and a first stage of experiments. No browser performance budget, real-weather fidelity, SF50 nonstandard-atmosphere calibration or adverse-weather hazard qualification follows from this research.

## 1. What weather is here

[P: FAA weather services and safety] The FAA distinguishes observations, forecasts and hazard reports. A terminal report describes an airport, not all air along a route. Cloud cover, visibility, wind shear, icing and turbulence affect flying in different ways. These distinctions motivate the scope below; the table itself is **[I]**, an engineering recommendation. It is not an aircraft operating procedure.

The user's choice is **include, later, or omit** for every row. Stage numbers recommend dependency order, not a commitment to include all phenomena. “Feels” includes aircraft acceleration and control response, not a claim of a motion platform.

[I] Recommended order is: **state/time/save contracts and mean profiles → temperature/pressure/humidity and visibility → coherent simple clouds plus validated gust/turbulence → precipitation → terrain-driven lift and surface history → storms/shear cells → aircraft icing and contamination**. Each stage supplies a dependency of the next. Detailed volumetric rendering can advance separately after the state/cloud contract; it must not block basic weather or change physics. Instrument weather should arrive with stage 1. Sound should follow each included physical phenomenon, within its own explicit budget.

[I] Two additional questions matter more than cloud appearance: **what time weather means** and **how uncertainty is shown**. A historical sun with current live weather is a deliberate mode, not a historical simulation. A cloud top inferred from a report must remain marked inferred even if its geometry looks precise. Both belong in the state and UI.

## 2. One state of the weather

### Recommended contract

[I] Use a versioned `WeatherWorld` with immutable source snapshots, a coarse resolved atmosphere and deterministic local features. Its definition is independent of the viewer and the aircraft. A revision is published atomically only when its required CPU field and visible representation are ready. A point query and a volume query refer to the same revision and UTC/elapsed simulation time.

| Quantity | Canonical unit / convention | Why it is present |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position | WGS84 latitude/longitude degrees; ellipsoid height m; explicitly converted MSL/geopotential/AGL heights | Prevent report cloud bases, model levels, terrain and aircraft from using different height zeros |
| Time | UTC epoch s plus integer physics step and dt s; model run, valid time, observation time, ingestion time separately | A forecast's issue time is not the air's valid time; replay cannot use arrival time |
| Mean wind | East/north/up m/s in local geodetic frame; renderer may transform to ECEF | Interpolate vectors, not compass angles; convert once to JSBSim north/east/down ft/s |
| Resolved gust/lift field | m/s, length m, lifetime s, feature ID and seed | Coherent cloud/event motion and aircraft encounters |
| Temperature | K; independent height coordinate | Arbitrary profiles, inversions and OAT |
| Pressure | Pa at the sampled height; surface pressure and reference datum retained | Density and altimetry; QNH/hPa is an aviation display/observation, not interchangeable with MSL pressure |
| Water vapor | Specific humidity kg vapor/kg moist air, or explicitly tagged mixing ratio kg/kg dry air | RH %, dew point K and moist density are derived, not independently contradictory controls |
| Cloud condensate | Liquid and ice mass concentration kg/m³; effective radius µm if known; phase | Connect optical extinction and icing potential; initially use a labelled optical proxy when mass is unavailable |
| Cloud geometry | Per-layer base/top m MSL, coverage fraction, kind/category, horizontal occupancy/shape parameters and seed | One definition for cloud entry, light/shadows and all rendering backends |
| Cloud optics | Extinction m⁻¹, single-scattering albedo fraction, phase asymmetry fraction; optical depth dimensionless | Optical depth is not cloud-cover fraction; link transmittance to light and visibility |
| Aerosols/fog | Extinction/scattering m⁻¹ and layer heights m; fog condensate separate where modeled | Local visibility/aerial perspective without changing planetary haze everywhere |
| Surface visibility observation | m, convention and censoring (e.g. “at least 10 statute miles”), uncertainty and station ID | Do not turn `10+`, `9999` or RVR into exact distance cutoffs |
| Precipitation | Liquid-equivalent mm/h at a named height; phase and particle/fall-speed parameters | Rate/accumulation intervals and solid depth differ; constrain near particles and distant shafts |
| Turbulence descriptor | Velocity variance m²/s² or explicitly named spectral model/intensity, length m, validity envelope | Aircraft subgrid response is derived, not a hidden extra world wind |
| Surface state | Water/snow/ice depth m, temperature K, elapsed accumulation/melt history and surface class | Wetness and contamination persist after rain stops |
| Hazard events | IDs, bounds, UTC start/duration s; cell winds/precipitation; lightning location/energy or photometric parameters | Deterministic storm, flash and thunder timing |
| Provenance | Provider/model/edition, field/level, run and validity, licence/credit, missing flags, observation/inference distinction, content hash | Save/replay and honest displays |

[I] Do not require a planetary 3D voxel grid. Keep the coarse provider field on its own grid, vertical coordinates and time slices. Manual weather is a spatially uniform column, optionally overlaid by authored regions; seeded weather is a reproducible coarse field plus feature catalogue. Use stable geographic cells to index local features. Renderer density textures are caches of the world definition, not a second simulator.

### Resolution, sampling and the cost of the contract

[I] For a first profile-based experiment use configurable **horizontal spacing 25 km**, **vertical output knots every 250 m below 3 km and 1 km above**, and **hourly coarse time slices**. These are starting controls, not added observational detail: retain native model levels and mark interpolated output. Manual columns need no horizontal samples. Use a separate **local feature spacing 100 m** and analytic bounded cloud shapes as initial experiments. Thin cloud/fog layers are represented by their exact base/top even when thinner than the coarse vertical grid.

[I] The proposal should compare native sampling with these candidate spacings before choosing defaults. A grid's spacing does not justify eddies, microbursts or cloud edges at that scale. HRRR at 3 km resolves more weather structure than GFS at 0.25°, but neither directly describes a gust striking an SF50 wing. Keep fine visual erosion from changing the physical occupancy boundary; if microstructure becomes physically significant, represent it in the canonical sampler too.

[I] Point sampling is allocation-free: retain surrounding grid cells and two valid-time slices, interpolate eight spatial corners and time, add the bounded nearby feature list, derive thermodynamics, and return into a caller-owned object. Cache cell/level search until the aircraft crosses a boundary. Use bilinear/trilinear interpolation of vectors and appropriately monotone scalars; interpolate pressure in log space. For irregular/hybrid model levels, convert the column to geometric altitude using its own pressure/temperature/geopotential information before the main loop. Reject below-terrain pressure levels. No JSON parsing, network, worker round-trip, terrain fetch, random allocation or grid decode belongs in a 120 Hz tick.

[I] At 120 Hz, three separate wind writes are **360 property writes/s**. Five atmosphere/wind writes are **600/s**; adding moisture/density adds more. The existing SDK's property batch can avoid repeated name lookups and crossings. Arithmetic and call counts are established, milliseconds are not: first-stage experiment E2 measures the sampler and adapter under normal concurrent machine use, subject to the repositories' timing policy. Do not claim that the entire weather field must be recomputed 120 times/s.

[I] Volume sampling exposes layer extents, feature IDs, coarse weather tiles and the identical analytic occupancy/extinction evaluator. Each backend implements the same coordinate/noise conventions or receives a baked canonical density tile. A CPU reference ray integrator verifies this correspondence. A floating-origin shift changes only coordinates used for rendering: it must not reseed weather or move cloud boundaries. Noise and feature IDs use stable geographic coordinates, with CPU float64 and bounded local GPU coordinates.

### Determinism and evolution

[I] Define randomness by hash of **world seed, algorithm version, geographic cell, feature ID and time interval**, rather than order of requests or render frames. Physical evolution follows the simulation clock, not wall-clock frame rate. Evaluate resolved feature trajectories against fixed checkpoints or integrate with a fixed world step independent of rendering. Uniform wind can advect a feature analytically; a varying field needs deterministic integration. Sheared layers can move at different winds; a tall coherent cloud needs an explicit deformation/velocity model, not independently sliding slices that tear apart.

[I] Clouds need the mean/resolved wind; aircraft Dryden fluctuations need not literally move a whole cloud at every 1/120 s. Specify this scale separation: resolved wind is shared, aircraft subgrid fluctuations are a trajectory response with recorded filter state. If several aircraft must encounter the same small eddy, use a deterministic spatial turbulence field instead of independent Dryden generators. This is a user scope decision, not something the current single-aircraft engine supplies.

[I] Live refresh has two distinct changes: movement of already accepted weather with simulation time, and assimilation of newly downloaded information. Publish a new revision with a **transition duration s** and effective simulation step; preserve feature IDs and interpolate thermodynamics/wind. Avoid instantly changing weather across the whole planet when the nearest station changes. Do not blend by opacity alone while physics jumps to the target cloud boundary. Prepare target data/geometry hidden, and publish matching CPU/GPU revisions together. If rendering is still being prepared, hold the previous accepted weather rather than letting the aircraft enter an invisible replacement cloud.

[I] Changes to Date and time invalidate valid-time selections, advection checkpoints and event schedules. Give the user separate modes: **weather follows scene UTC**, **weather pinned to a specified UTC**, or **manual/seeded conditions evolving from a specified epoch**. Live weather with a past sun should show both clocks. Offline replay pins accepted snapshots and evolution versions; no live fetch may silently replace them.

### How reports and grids become this state

[P: AWC API/FAA] METAR/TAF provide terminal observations/forecasts, not a full 3D atmosphere. TAF contains alternatives and time groups. [I] Normalize input into quantities plus uncertainty first; never feed a raw report directly to shader uniforms as the entire weather model.

| Input | Recommended conversion [I] | Gaps and policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| METAR station | Convert true “from” wind into vector; knots to m/s; station elevation and cloud bases from reporting datum to MSL; temperature/dew point to vapor; retain QNH and visibility censoring; cloud coverage brackets FEW/SCT/BKN/OVC, not precise density | Cloud tops, most cloud types, upper wind, fog depth, liquid water, turbulence and precipitation rate are missing. Use an explicitly selected profile/template or forecast prior, with every invented quantity marked inferred |
| TAF | Piecewise valid-time constraints; distinguish prevailing, TEMPO and probability groups | Do not average mutually exclusive weather into a fictitious continuous layer. User chooses deterministic branch/scenario or seeded realization with recorded probabilities and branch IDs |
| Winds aloft bulletin | Parse station, level, issue/validity and wind/temperature units | Sparse and often missing lowest levels; reconcile with surface observation; not a cloud/humidity forecast |
| Forecast grid | Retain u/v/w, temperature, pressure/height and moisture by native level/time; decode grid projection, vector orientation, missing bitmap and accumulated intervals in a worker/preparation step | Omega in Pa/s is not upward m/s: where hydrostatic approximation is accepted use `w_up = -omega/(rho*g)`; rotated winds must be transformed. Model cloud cover does not identify actual occupied subcells |
| Grid plus METAR | Forecast is the broad prior; observation adds a bounded, age-weighted local correction, fading with height/distance and available variables | Reconcile column hydrostatics after correction; do not impose station's pressure at all heights. Limit correction radius/age visibly; station selection must be stable through ties |
| Satellite/radar | Optional overlay or explicitly separate coarse constraint on cloud/precipitation footprint | No cloud-base/vertical-wind reconstruction from a colored image alone. Do not present an overlay as a coupled flight-weather model |
| No data, stale data or failure | Retain last accepted state with age/status; outside its footprint use selected manual/seeded baseline | No hidden calm reset, no invisible provider switch, and no forever-extrapolated cloud advection presented as live observation |

[I] For station corrections use compact smooth spatial weights, a height taper and age taper; start with user-visible candidates **radius 50 km**, **vertical influence 2 km**, **age limit 2 h**, **assimilation duration 120 s**. These are assumptions to test against the scope, not meteorological facts. Show exactly which station/model dominates. Forecast runs must remain individually identified when “best match” services stitch models together.

## 3. Where the weather comes from

### Recommendation and the static-site constraint

[I] Ship **manual and seeded weather as the baseline**. Both run offline, require no provider to learn where the view is, and can be saved exactly. If real weather is wanted, make it an explicit provider choice. For an initial direct-browser experiment, use **Open-Meteo JSON for a small profile/area** under its noncommercial service terms, or **user-imported normalized weather files** when commercial terms or privacy require it. For a later independent model pipeline investigate **NOAA GFS/HRRR S3 + worker decoding or preprocessing**, with openly reusable data. Use GIBS/radar as optional globe overlays first.

[I] “Live weather” should not mean “METAR-only”, “truth at every point”, or “automatic network requests”. Reports constrain surface conditions; models provide profiles and broad structure; deterministic synthesis fills subgrid and missing information. Show the blend and its age. Do not claim that aviation-grade fidelity follows from an aviation source.

| Source mode | What it supplies [I] | Costs and limits [I] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual | Full authored column, layers and explicit local events | User effort; no automatic connection to real conditions; easiest exact replay |
| Seeded | Reproducible regional fronts/profiles/cloud footprints from a versioned generator | Generator validation; plausible weather is not numerical forecasting; seed alone is insufficient after an algorithm changes |
| Reports only | Surface wind, visibility, clouds/ceiling and thermodynamic constraints at stations | Most volume is inferred; sparse/ocean coverage; METAR refresh and nearby-station changes must not teleport weather |
| Model plus reports | Broad 3D atmosphere with local corrections | Download, decoding, licences, model/observation mismatch, cache and blending; no resolved wing-scale turbulence |
| Imported snapshot | Any of the above prepared locally and loaded as a file | Import/format UX, provenance and bounded storage; no server; temporal coverage only what the file contains |
| Published prepared snapshots | Compressed geographic weather tiles and manifests on the existing static host | A local preparation script and manual republish; updates stop when the user stops publishing; bandwidth/hosting limits and attribution still apply |
| Proxy/subsetting service | Report CORS bridge, credential protection or model selection/regridding | A separately operated server/edge function, cache, quotas, maintenance and privacy policy. GitHub Pages cannot perform this work |

### Spatial wind and shear

[P: FGAuxiliary] Aerodynamic velocity is body velocity minus transformed total wind; aerodynamic angular velocity subtracts turbulent angular velocity. [I] Sample world wind immediately before each accepted engine step and write it through the existing public properties. Uniform steady wind changes relative airspeed without a fictitious imposed change to ground velocity. Crossing a real gradient changes relative flow and aerodynamic forces; the point-wind path captures those translational effects.

[P] The auxiliary alpha/beta derivative calculations use body acceleration without explicitly subtracting a local wind material derivative in the inspected expressions. [I] Therefore do not assume a rapidly changing point wind reproduces every unsteady aerodynamic derivative. The single point also misses differential flow across wings/tail, gust penetration and spatially varying rotation. Add these only through engine/aircraft models with tests, not a renderer-driven attitude kick. E2 compares a smooth analytic shear traversal, a temporal gust and an equivalent reference trajectory, including alpha-dot-sensitive coefficients where present.

[I] An observation refresh is not a physical shear. The world-state transition must prevent a data update from generating a zero-thickness wind discontinuity. Choose and show physical shear thickness, not a camera-distance fudge. A sensor reading of total wind comes from the engine/state at its physical location; an airport forecast remains a different reading.

### Turbulence choice, envelope and defects

[P: Yeager 1998 and installed source] Milspec and Tustin implement Dryden transfer-function formulations with low-altitude and high-altitude parameterizations, bridged between 1,000 and 2,000 ft. Installed Milspec uses explicit recurrences; Tustin uses bilinear/prewarped recurrences. [I] Recommend **Tustin as the candidate**, contingent on E3. It is suitable for stationary statistical encounters inside a documented aircraft/airspeed/height envelope. It does not infer convective storm turbulence, wake turbulence, thermals or terrain rotors from a METAR.

[I] For explicit recurrence `a = 1−dt/tau`, stability requires `0 < dt/tau < 2`; the second-order-related terms can be more restrictive. At 120 Hz, high airspeed/small lengths still need analysis, not “120 is enough”. Compare spectra, variance, correlations and angular rates at 60/120/240 Hz over sufficiently long stationary runs. The research's ten-second traces cannot establish these statistics. Check zero speed, tiny positive speed, very low AGL, altitude transitions, aircraft wingspan and severity changes. Tustin's `tan(dt/(2*tau))` also needs an envelope; prewarping does not make every input safe.

### Rain, icing and runway contamination

[I] First credible rain/snow model may be **visual + sound + visibility**, with aircraft dynamics declared unaffected. Do not add a universal rain drag/thrust penalty. Generic functionality belongs to JSBSim; aircraft configuration/effects belong to 0sfs.

[I] First icing release should be either **potential/exposure display** or a separately qualified aircraft model. The honest minimum is an authored exposure scenario with bounded aircraft-specific coefficient/system changes, explicitly approximate. [I] For wet/contaminated runway dynamics use a general wheel/contact law in JSBSim if needed, parameterized by surface depth/class, wheel speed/load and tire data; 0sfs supplies contact surface state and aircraft parameters. [P] **Bruneton/Neyret** provide precomputed spherical atmosphere/transport; **Hillaire 2020** provides scalable atmosphere LUTs and aerial perspective, switching technique for space views. **Frostbite 2016** describes cloud density, lighting, temporal integration and atmosphere/cloud composition. **Nubis 2018** presentation was downloaded and read (36 slides); the density model combines Perlin/Worley and erosion, with cheap/expensive sampling and light steps. **Nubis Evolved 2022** explicitly discusses fly-through clouds and temporal-artifact problems. These are distinct methods/versions; the early skybox results do not prove all of the later use cases or the three browser backends.

[I] Ray marching does not require compute. WebGL 2 provides fragment shaders and 3D texture sampling; Skybolt is direct evidence of a fragment implementation, although its desktop GLSL versions must be ported. WebGPU compute can generate density/cache data and schedule work, but a precomputed noise texture or CPU-generated density tile can serve WebGL 2. WebGL 1 can use 2D slices/atlases and bounded analytic fields, subject to precision/extensions; complex MRT/history or depth/float targets must not be assumed.

### Light, visibility and exposure

[I] Preserve the sky's clear-atmosphere transport as the baseline and add weather coefficients, avoiding duplicate haze/fog/cloud extinction. Along a path `T = exp(−∫ beta_ext ds)`. The renderer and CPU model use the same extinction, with scattering/source terms from the same Sun, Moon, sky and ground light. Incident illuminance is lux; outgoing cloud/surface radiance is cd/m² in the sky's three bands. Apply exposure/tone mapping once at final composition. A texture's alpha is coverage or transmittance only if defined that way; do not treat coverage fraction as optical depth.

[I] Direct ground light becomes clear-air direct illuminance × weather transmittance along the solar/lunar path. Diffuse sky illuminance requires angular integration of the weather-modified sky; overcast does not merely remove the Sun, because scattering redistributes light. Start with a CPU layer integration and a bounded multiple-scattering approximation; compare energy and standard overcast/cloud fixtures. Clouds must receive ground bounce under their current shadows. The rendered ground probe must see weather-modified imagery and lights; do not multiply its result by cloud attenuation a second time. Avoid a feedback loop that repeatedly adds yesterday's probe light to itself.

[I] The exposure meter samples **weather-modified** luminance/illuminance through its existing contract. A dull skybox plus a bright clear-sky terrain meter gives the wrong exposure. Mean cloud attenuation may serve a low-cost lighting approximation, but position-dependent ground shadows must use each ground location's weather, as the existing sky uses each location's Sun/Moon. No camera-local weather multiplier should darken the whole visible Earth uniformly.

[I] Visibility must specify a convention. For a chosen contrast threshold c, homogeneous extinction gives `V = −ln(c)/beta`; **c = 0.05 → 2.996/beta**, **c = 0.02 → 3.912/beta**. These are mathematical mappings, not proof that METAR prevailing visibility equals either formula. The proposal needs a MOR/prevailing-visibility/RVR policy and fixtures for object/background contrast, day/night lights, slant range and height-varying fog. A runway does not appear at an exact distance just because the report has that number; detection depends on contrast, lights and observer. Keep reported lower bounds and uncertainty, rather than a hard distance clip.

[I] Cloud brightness, ground lux and exposure can be checked against the CPU transport reference, but a model with artistic powder/multiple-scattering factors is not automatically physically calibrated. Skybolt's method is useful as a structure; its coefficients cannot be imported as the scale of luminance. Lightning introduces physical light and an exposure transient, with a user-controlled flash/motion/accessibility limit; recorded event identity must remain stable if the visible flash is reduced.

### Motion in an otherwise still view

[I] Clouds drifting, precipitation falling, a flash and changing illumination are visible changes, so they can legitimately request frames. They must not turn a calm static world into an unconditional frame loop. Schedule one frame for the next visible motion/error threshold or event, consolidate overlapping requests, stop when hidden/backgrounded and retain deterministic evolution evaluated at the next accepted time. Hidden cloud preparation and network progress request no frame; ready replacement content requests one.

[I] Expose animation cadence in **Hz**, projected-motion tolerance in **px**, and weather evolution speed as a dimensionless multiplier. A candidate globe default is **0 Hz additional weather-only frames** until the user enables motion; a flight can choose a host default because flight already draws moving aircraft. When a normal camera/flight frame is drawn, sample weather at its actual simulation time. A lower cadence reduces draw frequency, not the authoritative weather update rate. Frozen resolved weather is a separate deliberate world-time option that affects both volume and point queries. Disabling near-particle animation may remove particles while retaining extinction; it must not remove physical rain merely to save frames.

[I] Temporal reconstruction can request a **bounded number of convergence frames** after a change, then stop. Static clouds must not continue drawing just to change stochastic jitter. Motion vectors must include cloud advection/deformation, not just camera matrices. Reset/reject history for a time jump, world revision or origin transform that cannot be mapped exactly; store unclipped physical radiance or scale history consistently when exposure changes. Rain particles, lightning and fast near-cloud movement are particularly difficult to reconstruct. Their worst cases belong in E4, not a claim that temporal upscaling always saves work.

## 6. Sound, instruments, controls, saving and testing

### Sound and instruments

[P: local sound spec] Existing airframe wind sound follows airspeed/IAS, with its own gain; it is not environmental wind sound. The specification supplies no rain, thunder or ground-wind model. [I] Preserve that distinction: environmental wind uses world wind and nearby surfaces; airframe noise follows resulting air data. Rain/hail impacts need precipitation flux, relative velocity, hit surface and cockpit/exterior treatment. [I] Instruments consume JSBSim's resulting air data. ### Saving and replay

[P: local saved-flight source/spec] Saved-flight property filtering currently excludes `atmosphere/*`. The SF50 v2 proposal requires weather/seed export and generation-safe updates. [I] Keep the broad engine-property exclusion for ordinary aircraft snapshots, but add a separately versioned **world-weather record**: mode, source/valid times, epoch/time rate, coordinate/height datums, field and generator versions, seed, authored parameters, assimilated reports, bounded model subset or immutable content hashes plus accessible retained bytes, revision timeline and all included surface histories. [I] Separate three promises: (1) restore the same world weather, (2) replay the same aircraft inputs from a known initial state, (3) resume every internal stochastic filter bit-for-bit. [I] CPU checks can establish units/datums; meteorological direction conversion; hydrostatic/thermodynamic consistency; moisture conversions; interpolation bounds; continuity at tile/height/time boundaries; seeded field equality across sampling order; allocation-free sampler behaviour; cloud entry/exit and precipitation-source overlap; extinction integration and analytic contrast-distance cases; advection and mass/flux bookkeeping within declared approximations; lightning/thunder timelines; source decoding/provenance; cache caps/cancellation; generation-safe publication; export/replay hashes; and idle scheduler requests. Sparse reports and local detail need additional representations. Section 2 proposes bounded fields with explicit datums, not a compulsory planet-sized grid |
| Cache interpolation weights until crossing a grid boundary | Corrected [I]: cell indices/corner values can be cached; weights change continuously within the cell as position changes. Regular-grid interpolation already takes a bounded number of samples; a full effect-volume cache is not inherently necessary |
| IDW or nearest-station Voronoi produces smooth transitions | Corrected [I]: nearest-station assignment creates boundaries; IDW alone does not impose hydrostatic/moisture consistency, altitude influence or temporal continuity. Use bounded corrections with explicit blending, uncertainty and tests |

| JSON plus seed ensures identical replay | Corrected [I]: generator/adapter/engine versions, canonical revisions, inputs/events and stochastic filter history also matter. Section 6 distinguishes world restore, replay and exact resume |
| `T-R`, `P-psf`, `rho-slugs_ft3` are writable | Corrected [P/O]: installed source binds getters only. Working point inputs are `override/temperature`, `override/pressure`, `override/density`; their units/order/consistency are in section 4 and WASM evidence |
| Type 1 provides basic randomness; type 0 fully disables turbulence | Corrected [P/O]: installed type 1 has no active switch case; switching to 0 leaves angular turbulence in the observed case. Upstream fixes and regressions precede qualification |
| Forecast gusts map directly to Dryden severity or a cosine event | Not established [I]: a gust statistic does not specify a spectrum, probability curve, event duration/direction or length scale. Such a mapping requires a declared model and independent validation |
| Browser stalls require extrapolated wind decaying to zero after 500 ms | Rejected [I]: world sampling follows simulated time at each successful fixed step, not wall-clock stalls. Hardware-dependent decay breaks replay and adds artificial shear. Driver catch-up/pause policy must not rewrite weather |
| Setting local T/P automatically gives correct turbine hot-day performance | Not established [P/O]: actual engine XML depends on several inputs, including `delta-T` not updated by point overrides. Existing calibration and concurrent redesign need a consistent ambient contract and E3 |
| Humidity/temperature thresholds and coefficient changes are minimum credible icing | Corrected [I]: humidity is not supercooled liquid-water content; collection/accretion, aircraft geometry/protection and held-out aerodynamic evidence are needed. A generic penalty can only be a labelled authored scenario |
| Rain state should directly reduce runway friction | Corrected [I]: current rain is not accumulated surface water/contamination; wheel/surface/load/speed conditions matter. Empirical contact laws and surface history are needed |
| Dew-point spread above 25,000 ft credibly determines contrails | Not established [I]: exhaust/mixing and ice-saturation conditions are omitted. Contrails are an additional aircraft-related scope decision, not the definition of atmospheric humidity |
| Thermals/waves from a downward terrain ray and cylinders are credible | Limited [I]: cached terrain gradients can support a labelled ridge-lift approximation; mountain waves need stratification and wind profiles, and thermal fields need compensating sink and lifecycle. | A plane/screen rain/full-screen flash is minimum complete weather | Corrected [I]: these can be visual effects, but do not establish in-cloud occupancy, precipitation source/flux or localized lightning lighting. Shared physical state and honest capability labels are still required |
| `OD = exp(−K Δz)` is optical depth | Corrected [I, derivation]: optical depth is `τ = ∫β ds`; for constant extinction, `τ = K Δs`. Transmittance is `exp(−τ)`, not optical depth. Path length follows the light/view direction, not always vertical thickness |
| `L = C ΔT(1−ΔT)` is accumulated ray light | Corrected [I, derivation]: for constant source radiance over a segment, contribution is `T_before × C × (1−T_segment)`; then update `T_after = T_before × T_segment`. Arbitrary units for C or replacing cumulative transmittance with segment transmittance loses consistency |
| Multiply all ground illuminance by cloud transmittance | Corrected [I]: attenuation applies along each incident-light path; diffuse sky/cloud multiple scattering also contributes. Direct Sun/Moon attenuation alone does not determine overcast lux/exposure |
| Cloud shadows require a top-down pass; Skybolt relies on compute | Corrected [P/I]: inspected Skybolt uses fragment shaders. A shadow texture/pass is one option; directional light-path integration or precomputed mean attenuation are others. Choice needs height/backend/light tests |
| Floating origin means camera is always exactly zero | Not established [I]: floating origin need not equal camera origin. Preserve stable world/cell identity and use relative/high-precision coordinates; moving noise with the origin would change the weather |
| Continuous animation requires changing render-on-demand architecture | Corrected [I]: visible evolution is a change already covered by the scheduler. Explicit motion Hz/error bounds schedule necessary frames; frozen views incur none. No compulsory 5/10 Hz loop follows from a cloud method |
| 128³ RGBA8 ≈8 MB and 32³ RGBA8 ≈128 KB | Confirmed arithmetic [I]: 8 MiB and 128 KiB for one level, excluding histories, staging, other volumes and replacement peaks |
| Tight numeric bounds/defaults are physical requirements | Not established [I]: bounds must cover chosen flight/globe envelope and source data; e.g. pressure below 1,000 Pa exists above the proposed minimum. Resource controls must not silently clamp the canonical physical state |

The decoder, shader and interpolation leads remain candidates for E1/E2/E4; source code, licences and named-device evidence would have to be inspected before use.
